Website monitoring is the practice of automatically checking whether your website is working correctly — and alerting you when it isn't. If your website goes offline at 2am on a Sunday, do you want to find out from a customer complaint on Monday morning, or from an alert sent to your phone within minutes?

What does website monitoring check?

Modern website monitoring covers much more than simple availability. A complete monitoring setup checks:

  • Uptime — is your website responding to requests?
  • Response time — how fast is it responding?
  • SSL certificate — is your HTTPS certificate valid and not about to expire?
  • DNS records — are your domain name settings correct?
  • Email security — are SPF, DKIM and DMARC configured properly?
  • Security headers — does your site send the right HTTP security headers?

Why does website monitoring matter?

Search engines like Google factor in website availability and speed as ranking signals. A website that is frequently offline or slow to load will rank lower in search results — meaning fewer customers finding you.

Beyond SEO, every minute your website is down is a minute where potential customers hit an error page and leave, probably to a competitor. For an e-commerce store or a booking system, website downtime directly costs money.

How does monitoring work?

A monitoring service like Presence sends automated requests to your website at regular intervals (typically every minute to every hour, depending on your plan). If your website fails to respond, or responds with an error, the monitoring service immediately sends you an alert by email, push notification, or another channel you have configured.

Beyond availability, monitoring also runs periodic scans of your domain's DNS records, SSL certificate, and email security configuration — and calculates a Website Health Score that gives you a single number summarising your website's overall health.
