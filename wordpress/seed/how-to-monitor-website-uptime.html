Uptime monitoring tracks whether your website is accessible to visitors. The goal is simple: know the moment your website goes down, so you can act quickly and minimise the impact on your business.

What is uptime?

Uptime refers to the percentage of time your website is accessible and responding correctly. A website with 99% uptime is still down for about 7.3 hours per month — which may be acceptable for a personal blog, but problematic for a restaurant that takes online bookings.

How uptime monitoring works

An uptime monitoring service sends an HTTP request to your website every 1–5 minutes. If your website responds with a success status code (like 200 OK), it records the check as successful. If it times out, returns an error, or is unreachable, an incident is triggered and alerts are sent immediately.

What affects your uptime?

  • Server issues — your hosting provider's servers can go down or become overloaded.
  • DNS problems — misconfigured DNS records can make your domain unreachable.
  • SSL certificate expiry — expired certificates cause browsers to block your site.
  • Traffic spikes — unexpected traffic can overwhelm servers on lower-tier hosting plans.
  • Software updates gone wrong — a CMS or plugin update can break your site.

What to do when your website goes down

When you receive a downtime alert, the first step is to confirm it — visit your site from a different device or network. Then contact your hosting provider's support if it is a server-side issue, or check your DNS settings and recent plugin updates if the issue is on your end.
