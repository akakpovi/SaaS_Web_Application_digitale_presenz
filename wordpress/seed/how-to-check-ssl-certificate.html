An SSL certificate (technically a TLS certificate) proves your website's identity and encrypts the connection between your visitors' browsers and your server. Without one — or with an expired one — browsers display scary "Not Secure" warnings that drive visitors away.

Why SSL certificates expire

SSL certificates are issued with a fixed validity period — currently capped at 398 days. When a certificate expires, browsers immediately start showing security warnings. In Chrome, visitors see a red warning page with "Your connection is not private" — most people leave immediately.

How to check your SSL certificate

The quickest way is to use our free SSL Certificate Checker. Enter your domain and you will instantly see:

  • Whether your certificate is valid and trusted.
  • The expiry date and how many days remain.
  • The issuing certificate authority.
  • Whether the certificate covers your domain correctly.

What to do if your certificate is expiring

Most modern hosting providers handle SSL certificate renewal automatically using Let's Encrypt. Log in to your hosting control panel and check that automatic renewal is enabled. If you are using a paid certificate, you will need to purchase a renewal and install it before the current one expires.

Set up automated monitoring with Presence and you will receive an alert 30 days before your certificate expires — giving you plenty of time to renew it.
